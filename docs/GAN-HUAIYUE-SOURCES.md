# Huai–Yue Gan: Xu Jian’s settlement comparisons

This pack publishes **106 source-attested character readings at nine named settlements**, with 18 sound notes and 18 documented cultural topics. It is not a conversational vocabulary course, an audio collection, or a claim that a settlement has one uniform accent. Four slash alternatives retain their source register labels. Twenty further readings remain held for geography; no sound was filled in or reassigned to a nearby place.

[Publisher PDF](https://geolinguistics.sakura.ne.jp/Monograph/SiG-mono2-2-xia-ebook.pdf), [Zenodo record](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.6342364). The publisher deposit specifies **CC BY 4.0**. Exact PDF SHA256 is recorded in `gan-huaiyue-provenance.json`; the licence metadata was checked through the [Zenodo API](https://zenodo.org/api/records/6342364).

The tables compare several language groups. Only Gan rows explicitly situated by the article’s prose are included. No Wu, Hui, Jianghuai Mandarin, reconstructed forms, or tone-omitting segment lists were imported. Source pp262 and266 describe the regional distribution; pp264–268 contain the selected tables.

## Pronunciation and learning scope

- All rows are `learningKind: character-reading` with labels such as “Character 狗”. No English lexical sense is invented from a character table; these readings must remain outside meaning-based vocabulary quizzes.
- Superscript digits are source pitch contours. Single `5` on Erlang checked syllables stays pitch level5; it is never converted into tone category5.
- Source `ә` U+04D9 is normalized to IPA `ə` U+0259 where present. This is a typography normalization only; every original segment string and cell survives in provenance and the reading note. Other IPA symbols and aspiration remain unchanged. Pitch digits are rendered as superscripts without changing values.
- Footnote1 p264 explicitly defines the first slash alternative as colloquial and the second as literary, unless otherwise stated. Yaodu 头 and Erlang 额 each retain both labelled alternatives. No register is assigned to unslashed cells.
- Table5 identifies 特 in 特为 and 肋 in 肋条. Only the character’s reading is supplied. Neither complete compound pronunciation nor a whole-word meaning is generated.
- Meicheng’s 特 has `tʰɛ13`, and 肋 has `liɛ42`. These were independently image-checked; seemingly unusual contours are not “repaired” to match other rows.
- p269 describes a very short transitional i in Meicheng. The pack preserves the broader table transcription rather than inventing a narrower form.
- p263 says unmarked data come from the author’s investigation. The paper does not supply collection dates or speaker details for these selected settlements. Erlang is explicitly reproduced from Sun Yizhi2002. The date2022 is publication, not an inferred recording date. These qualifications appear beside every reading.
- No audio or phonetic place-name reading is supplied. HanLingo spelling is generated by the shared IPA converter only. The ordinary catalogue labels are not advertised as attested local endonyms.

## Classification, geography and comparable reference scope

The new points reuse `gan/huaiyue/huaiyue-localities`, an existing **geographic collection**. No formal linguistic subbranch is invented for website depth.

Xu2022 establishes Gan membership of each selected settlement. Huai–Yue branch alignment is cross-checked against Jing Liwen2025’s [second-edition Atlas county distribution dataset](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.15897647), CC BY4.0. Exact county row numbers and source fields appear in provenance. Dongzhi contains Gan, Mandarin and Hui references; its Yaodu study is included because Xu explicitly places it in the Gan comparison, not because every Dongzhi resident is presumed Gan-speaking.

Coordinates come separately from **Wikidata P625**, with exact Chinese settlement label and direct **P131 county identity**; they are approximate town anchors, not author-collected speaker locations. The source paper’s map is schematic and is not used for precise geocoding. Raw query results and SHA256 are identified in the ledger. Meicheng and Tiantang have two nearby coordinate statements within1km; the existing value with greater decimal precision is selected without averaging. All alternatives are retained.

Existing Gan/Xiang entries for these counties are explicitly administrative distribution references. The new references concern source-named settlements, so they do not overwrite or relabel those county records. No place-name IPA is inferred from the common map name.

## Held material

All126 extracted candidates remain in provenance; only106 are emitted.

- **太湖羊河 Yanghe —14 readings:** current records identify a village under Niuzhen. An independently verified village coordinate and exact historical source-site reconciliation are still missing. The Niuzhen town centre is not substituted.
- **太湖晋熙 Jinxi —6 readings:** Wikidata Q31852669 supplies two equally precise anchors over2km apart, with old/new urban scope unresolved. Neither is silently selected.

These are geographic holds, not assertions that the phonetic source is wrong. Releasing them requires evidence and a reviewed ledger update.

## Culture

Culture entries cite specific museums, buildings, crafts, performances or dated community events in each town or its named villages. Sources are municipal/heritage records, direct institutional reports and attributed local reporting. Their full titles and URLs are kept beside the notes and in provenance. Museum collections can describe a wider region; performances have their own pronunciation conventions. None is used as evidence of a phonetic survey speaker’s address, identity or beliefs.

No photographs are added by this pack. Cultural notes do not claim gallery parity or vocabulary completion.
