# Min branch learning evidence

Verified 9 October 2026. These packs extend Eastern Min, Northern Min, Puxian, and Central Min. They do not replace the detailed Amoy lesson pack.

## Pronunciation

- **Fuzhou: 58 character readings.** [CUHK Fuzhou syllable index](https://humanum.arts.cuhk.edu.hk/Lexis/lexi-mf/dialectIndex.php?point=O), rows 210, 632, 726, 912, 1070, 1216, 1424. The university database supplies IPA segments and pitch contours separately. We join those fields, render pitch digits as IPA Chao tone letters, and expand the legacy affricate ligature ʦ to t͡s. We do not infer phrases or sandhi. 茶 ta53, 水 tsuoi32, 魚 ŋy53, 米 mi32, 手 tshieu32, 山 saŋ44, 三 saŋ44, 日 niʔ5.
- **Jian’ou: 60 character readings.** [CUHK Jian’ou syllable index](https://humanum.arts.cuhk.edu.hk/Lexis/lexi-mf/dialectIndex.php?point=Q), rows 287, 376, 620, 932, 1301, 1351, 1358, 1366. 茶 ta33, 水 sy21, 魚 ŋy33, 米 mi42, 人 neiŋ33, 手 siu21, 山 suiŋ54, 三 saŋ54. These are selected character readings, not an assertion that every character has only one reading. The database includes alternate readings for some other characters; those were not flattened into one invented norm.
- **Putian: 29 attested forms.** [Wu 2021, Puxian subgrouping](https://www.ling.sinica.edu.tw/upload/researcher_manager_result/17e7b4c054664b1e85670eb38147af5b.pdf), printed pp. 160–164, tables 1–5. Readings transcribed from the Putian row only. Table 4 explicitly maps category 1→533, 2→13, 3→453, 5→42, 6→11, 8→4. Those mappings are disclosed in the UI. ts and tsh are normalized to t͡s and t͡sʰ; plain h used as aspiration in the source becomes superscript ʰ. PDF pages 6–8 were rendered and visually checked to avoid text-extraction errors. The source's [ɬ] must not become [l] or [s].
- **Yong’an: 20 dictionary readings; source added in this expansion.** [Wu and Lin 2007](https://www.ling.sinica.edu.tw/upload/researcher_manager_result/80f9788d396d35b0e8c32ebd19416ac5.pdf), pp. 1–2, visually checked. This is comparative historical evidence of endings, not a contemporary phrasebook. Those comparative tables remain background sound notes; the new word readings instead come from Zhou and Lin’s local monograph, detailed below.

CUHK's separate character definitions supply the ordinary English glosses; the cards are explicitly labelled dictionary character readings. No generated speech or locally hosted recording is claimed. CUHK links include their own speaker controls: direct Fuzhou audio endpoint `dialect/O/O00002.mp3` was verified as HTTP 200 audio/mpeg. Their recordings remain on the university site; we do not redistribute them. We did not substitute Singapore Fuzhou for Fuzhou city or Fuqing for Fuzhou.

## Further study

[Peng, A Phonetic Study of Fuzhou Chinese](https://lbms03.cityu.edu.hk/theses/c_ftt/phd-ctl-b4086098xf.pdf) was retrieved and its table 2.1 on printed p. 11 visually inspected. Its experiments distinguish citation forms, speaker measurements, and connected tones. We link the study but do not mix its experimental contours with the CUHK word set.

[Wu 2023](https://www.ling.sinica.edu.tw/upload/researcher_manager_result/c37535b570fa1f44797850580f9ef3ba.pdf), pp. 73–77, supplies Northern Min rhyme correspondences. Its numbered categories were not mistaken for pitch contours. The older [Lien abstract](https://thjcs.site.nthu.edu.tw/p/406-1452-41564%2Cr2998.php?Lang=zh-tw) describes competing Jian’ou rhyme systems, but the accessible page was not a full-text word source.

- [Jian’ou banner balancing](https://www.ihchina.cn/Article/Index/detail?id=13779) is documented by the national intangible-heritage registry. Only equipment, movement, and musical-accompaniment facts are paraphrased; no unsupported origin narrative is reproduced.
- [Mazu belief and customs](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/mazu-belief-and-customs-00227) is UNESCO's primary entry and includes a film. The location is explicitly Meizhou Island within Putian, not a claim that the pictured ritual or speech represents the urban speech of every Putian resident.
- [Yong’an Daqiang opera](https://www.ihchina.cn/project_details/13157/) is located in Fengtian, Qingshui She township. We explicitly distinguish its performance tradition from urban Yong’an conversation.

## Yong’an: closing the first-word gap

The accessible [scan of **永安方言**](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/ss-186173002/186173002) is the primary monograph by **周长楫 (Zhou Changji) and 林宝卿 (Lin Baoqing)**, edited by 永安市地方志编委会 and published by 厦门大学出版社. Cover and ISBN page (7-5615-0380-6) were visually inspected, along with printed pages 5–8, 21–29. The scan host’s generated description and OCR were not used as pronunciation evidence. The prefaces date from 1990; these cards are a documented dictionary reference, not a claim of newly recorded speech.

- **Tone mapping:** printed p. 7 gives 1→42, 2→33, 3→21, 4→54, 5→24, 6→12. Printed pp. 8 and 22 confirm that the small digits on headwords are category numbers, not contours. We convert only using that same book’s table.
- **Printed p. 23:** 爸 pa1, 來 la2, 奶 la3, 菜 tsʰa5, 才 tsa2, 財 tsa2, 再 tsa5, 太 tʰa3.
- **Printed p. 28:** 買 be3, 賣 be5, 雪 se6, 洗 se3, 細 se5.
- **Printed p. 29:** 米 bi3, 四 si5, 紙 ti3, 鼻 pʰi5, 七 tsʰi6, 皮 pʰi2, 比 pi3.

The 20 readings preserve the book’s distinction between unaspirated, aspirated and voiced stops. Aspiration written with an apostrophe in the printed notation is rendered as IPA [ʰ], and ts as [t͡s]. Simplified headwords in the scan are displayed in the project’s traditional character style. Each card retains its own source link and citation-reading qualification. We excluded the monograph’s sinological apical-vowel symbol, phonetic ambiguities in its broad vowel notation, and entries with unclear print; no new IPA value was guessed. Three new sound notes teach the attested onset contrast and tone pairs, bringing Yong’an to five sound notes.
