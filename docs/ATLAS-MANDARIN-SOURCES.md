# Mandarin locality catalogue

The catalogue has 103 locality references in eight branches and 24 documented clusters. It preserves the 13 existing Mandarin learning-place IDs. These are source-backed reference places, not 103 completed vocabulary lessons. No IPA, tone contours, local endonyms, sound recordings, or photographs were inferred for the added places.

The four displayed linguistic levels are **Mandarin → branch → cluster → locality**. Lower source subdivisions remain in reference notes rather than becoming extra navigation levels. Coordinates are approximate urban or town-center anchors, not dialect boundaries, survey-speaker locations, or claims that all residents speak alike. Familiar English geographic names are retained when a sourced local name is unavailable. Source spelling is not HanLingo romanization.

## Classification baseline and corrections

[Xiong Zhenghui and Zhang Zhenxing, 汉语方言的分区, 方言 2008(2):97–108](https://ling.cass.cn/keyan/xueshuchengguo/cgtj/202112/W020211223385125636799.pdf) describes the revised Atlas divisions. Its pp. 101–106 establish the branch and cluster scheme used here. In particular:

- Beijing has **Jing–Cheng and Chao–Feng**. Jingshi and Huaicheng become lower subdivisions of Jing–Cheng; they are not parallel full clusters. Shike is cancelled: its Xinjiang points are reassigned to Lan–Yin or Central Plains Mandarin.
- Northeastern has Ji–Shen, Ha–Fu and Hei–Song. The explicit additions on p. 101 support Arxan, Genhe and Ergun. Jianchang is reassigned away from Beijing; it is not copied into our Beijing list.
- Jiao–Liao uses Qing–Lai, not the former Qingzhou cluster name, alongside Deng–Lian and Gai–Huan.
- Central Plains assignments follow the p. 102 revisions: Lingbao and Sanmenxia in Guanzhong; Hancheng, Heyang and Dali in Fenhe; Linxia, Hezheng and Guanghe in Hezhou.
- Anqing, Tongcheng and Zongyang belong to Jianghuai's Huang–Xiao cluster under the p. 103 revision, rather than Hong–Chao.
- Chengyu is a lower subdivision of Southwestern Mandarin's Chuan–Qian cluster; Wuhan's Ezhong is a lower subdivision of Huguang.

The earlier Hebei survey below is used for named localities only where its membership remains consistent with the revised scheme. An earlier label is not silently promoted to a current cluster rank. Only clusters with sourced locality references are currently exposed; this is not an exhaustive inventory of every Mandarin cluster.

## Verification and limitations

Each exported locality has its own source URL, locator, branch, cluster, Chinese name, geographic anchor and scope. Existing lesson IDs remain stable: `beijing-city`, `harbin`, `shenyang`, `jinan`, `qingdao`, `dalian`, `zhengzhou`, `xian`, `lanzhou`, `nanjing`, `chengdu`, `chongqing`, `wuhan`.

CASS PDF downloads failed during this pass; their searchable, indexed article text was inspected with targeted page and phrase queries. The Liaoning article was inspected in its author-uploaded full-text copy. Source access dates and web-host timestamps are not substituted for publication dates. OCR excerpts were used to locate classification statements, never to reconstruct lesson IPA. Every catalogue source remains linked for review.

Coverage is deliberately uneven where evidence is uneven: Southwestern has four supported references here. More places require individual source work. A catalogue record does not enable Words, Sounds, Photos or Practice without the separate learning-pack evidence required by `docs/BRANCH-LEARNING.md`. This expansion does not claim new local vocabulary, recordings, galleries, mutual-intelligibility measurements, or dialect boundaries.
