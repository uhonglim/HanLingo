# Jiangyong · Chengguan: twelve character readings

This starter contains eleven character readings and one explicitly glossed local address term, not a conversation course or a recording collection. Its locality is **Chengguan, the Jiangyong county town**, separately recorded from Baishui village and Shangjiangxu. The canonical route is `/tuhua/southern-hunan/yongzhou-area/jiangyong-chengguan`.

## Primary pronunciation source

Huang Xuezhen 黄雪贞, *江永方言研究* (*Jiangyong Fangyan Yanjiu*), Social Sciences Academic Press, June 1993, ISBN 9787800503879. [Public catalogue and scan](https://archive.org/details/jiangyong-fangyan-yanjiu-9787800503879).

The author began Jiangyong work in 1986 and chose Chengguan for the **focused 1987 survey**. The publication year is **1993**, not the collection date. The afterword is dated August 1992. Source p. 1 names the county seat and distinguishes Chengguan Tuhua from the region’s externally used Southwest Mandarin. No age, sex, recording, or present-day representativeness is inferred for this selection.

The tone key gives 44, 42, 35, 13, 21, 33 and short 5. Source tone marks are rendered as superscript pitch digits, not converted from historical class numbers. Short 5 stays one digit; the key does not authorize adding a glottal stop or any other coda. The twelve selected readings happen to cover the first six contours, not short 5.

Eleven entries are `learningKind: character-reading`, with neutral English labels `Character <han>` rather than inferred local meanings. Any generic written-character dictionary sense is a separate, independently attributed aid. 奶 alone is `learningKind: word`: the source explicitly defines the grandmother address term, so its English is “grandmother; used in address”. The homophone table is not an everyday word list. Two entry-specific restrictions remain visible: 奶 means grandmother in address in this entry; 飞 is illustrated with 飞机 and cross-refers an additional reading which has not been transcribed into this starter. No literary/colloquial label is assigned where the selected source entry has no such mark.

HanLingo spelling is generated by the shared converter from these IPA values. No independent source romanization or speculative pronunciation of the locality name is supplied. Nüshu Unicode common readings and the separate Shangjiangxu community are not imported into this pack.

## Geography and naming

The separate [pinned Sincomp row for 江永城關(市區)](https://github.com/lernanto/sincomp/blob/537236686ac30938aff807c4858de5b8d5c24e73/src/sincomp/ccr_dialects.csv#L516) supplies longitude 111.345900, latitude 25.271960. This is an approximate urban map anchor, not a surveyed speaker address. The preceding row names Baishui and is deliberately not used. The CSV’s download/index fields do not constitute available pronunciation evidence; the readings come from Huang’s book.

[Jiangyong County’s administrative history](https://www.jiangyong.gov.cn/jiangyong/xzqh/list_tt.shtml) records Chengguan town’s return to the name Xiaopu in June 1995. This locates the historical name without equating Huang’s urban sample with all of today’s expanded Xiaopu administrative area. “Jiangyong · Chengguan” preserves the source locality; Xiaopu is a searchable alias. A verified local IPA name remains unavailable.

The existing `southern-hunan/yongzhou-area` hierarchy is geographic. It is not presented as a newly discovered linguistic subgroup or a classification adopted from Huang.

## Two county-town culture topics

[Liu Yue-bing’s reported Xiaopu Street visit, Hunan Daily, 20 July 2022](https://m.voc.com.cn/xhn/news/202207/13980188.html) directly locates both subjects in the county town: wooden steelyard-making in a working shop, and Wenchang Pavilion by the Yongming River. The short English descriptions are original factual summaries; no article text or photos are republished. The pavilion is not the similarly named one at Shanggantang. These subjects provide geographic cultural context, not evidence of the interviewees’ language or ancestry.
