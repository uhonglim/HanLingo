# Mandarin and Yue learning sources

Checked 9 October 2026. Data: `src/data/learning/mandarin-yue.ts`.

## Coverage

All seven existing branches have locality-scoped sound notes, cultural context and further study links. Jinan supplies ten IPA character readings, Nanjing nine, Hong Kong six tone examples and Yulin twelve kinship roots: 37 readings in total. Chengdu and Guangzhou reuse the existing licensed photographs, each only in its documented locality. No Standard Mandarin word has been relabelled as local Beijing speech.

The learning packs are an expansion of verified material, not a complete course in every variety. Empty Beijing and Chengdu word arrays intentionally suppress vocabulary/practice destinations; no invented readings are added to fill them.

## Lexical evidence

### Jinan and Nanjing

[CUHK Jinan table](https://humanum.arts.cuhk.edu.hk/Lexis/lexi-mf/dialectIndex.php?point=B) and [Nanjing table](https://humanum.arts.cuhk.edu.hk/Lexis/lexi-mf/dialectIndex.php?point=D) were downloaded and their character rows inspected. Initial, final and the numeric contour printed after the tone-category label are concatenated without changing any segment or contour. Nasalization and the Nanjing [ɯ] are preserved. Every card links to the exact character entry. Jinan offers ten everyday characters; Nanjing offers nine. Nanjing 人 has two rows and was omitted rather than choosing an unexplained variant.

The HTML includes recording controls, for example Jinan 茶 points to `dialect/B/B00731.mp3`; dictionary resources identify those recordings without bundling audio. Jinan’s CUHK Tone 4 value 21 differs from the Huang paper’s 41. The lesson explicitly attributes both instead of silently harmonizing them.

### Hong Kong

M. Chan, _Alveolarization in Hong Kong Cantonese: a sociophonetic study of neogrammarian and lexical diffusion models of sound change_, Oxford DPhil, 2017. [Repository record](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A2d40e687-83cd-4d93-9c3e-fa6e5569cf6b), Table 21, p. 78.

Six rows: 思 /siː55/, 史 /siː35/, 試 /siː33/, 時 /siː21/, 市 /siː24/, 事 /siː22/. The table gives IPA tone letters and corresponding numeric pitch values. HanLingo uses those exact numeric values, including 24 for the low rising tone; it does not replace this with 23 from another description. The table was verified through indexed full-text retrieval; direct PDF download returned 403 during this audit. The repository record identifies the author, year and CC BY license.

The SFUSED corpus is linked for its documented recordings and phonological discussion, not copied as strict IPA. Its transcription uses adapted Jyutping/IPA and represents aspiration with voiced/voiceless letter pairs. No raw SFUSED words were imported into the strict IPA cards.

### Yulin

Wenmin Hu, _The Comparison of Kinship Terminology in the Yulin Dialect and in Cantonese_, 2020. [Article](https://pressto.amu.edu.pl/index.php/linpo/article/view/linpo-2020-0001), [full PDF](https://pressto.amu.edu.pl/index.php/linpo/article/download/linpo-2020-0001/28611).

Downloaded and inspected full PDF; Table 1, pp. 11–12. Scope is Yuzhou and Fumian, not every locality administered by Yulin. Twelve basic relation roots were selected. The source’s Chinese character forms and IPA segments/pitch digits are retained. These are roots rather than a list of complete conversational address forms: prefixes and changed tones occur in fuller terms. 婆 is used for both maternal and paternal grandmother in the source table; its card says so.

The article explicitly uses **IPA for Yulin** and **Jyutping for Cantonese**. Only the Yulin column was imported. Converting the Cantonese column silently to IPA would erase that distinction. The source has a CC BY-NC-ND license; no PDF or facsimile is redistributed. The site cites a small selection of lexical facts with original explanatory prose, not the entire table.

## Mandarin evidence and limits

- **Beijing**: Han Hu’s 2022 LOT dissertation on rhotacization in the Beijing speech community. Full PDF retrieved; font encoding complicates reliable text extraction. No guessed rhotic words were added. Local Beijing and the standard remain distinct.
- **Ji–Lu / Jinan**: Yujing Huang’s two-page paper on position-sensitive Tone 4 sandhi, hosted by the University of Barcelona. Full PDF verified. It describes citation 41 and penultimate sandhi, without a complete word/IPA vocabulary table.
- **Jianghuai / Nanjing**: Xin Li’s LOT dissertation 527, Chapter 2 and Appendix A. Full PDF verified. It documents five lexical tones, earlier differences in contour descriptions, and an experiment with 18 young adult Nanjing speakers. Appendix A supplies characters, pinyin and glosses, **not IPA**. The four Tone 1 characters are therefore presented as a source reading exercise, not IPA cards.
- **Southwestern / Chengdu**: Hai Hu and Yiwen Zhang, _Path of Vowel Raising in Chengdu Dialect of Mandarin_, arXiv:1803.03887. Full PDF verified. The 17-speaker experiment supports context- and age-sensitive vowel raising. Its labels `pan1`, `pan2`, `pan3` refer to different experimental characters; those digits are not tone contours. No lexical cards were made from these labels.

- Nanjing: UNESCO’s Yunjin brocade entry, including its two-person loom technique.
- Chengdu: the existing CC0 Daderot Baihuatan Park teahouse photograph. No claim about the identity or language of pictured people.
- Guangzhou: the existing CC BY-SA 2.5 wingmelee photograph of Guangzhou Youth Cantonese Opera Troupe; source/license remain attached.
- Hong Kong: UNESCO’s Cantonese opera account. The Guangzhou image is not reused as a Hong Kong photograph.
- Siyi / Taishan: UNESCO’s Qiaopi/Yinxin nomination, section 3.4, specifically identifying Wuyi’s regional correspondence terminology. Regional scope is explicit; no Kaiping photograph is labelled Taishan.
- Yulin: the linguistic study’s distinction between direct and indirect address supplies culture through actual family vocabulary, without an unrelated generic photograph.

## External resource limitations

- The old CUHK URL for Cheng’s _The Phonology of Taishan_ returned 404. The pack uses the verified JSTOR article record instead and states access may be required. The encyclopedia source record was also updated to this verified URL during integration.
- `taishandict.com` is a community dictionary with audio and a published transcription key. Its simplified phonetic notation is explicitly identified, rather than presented as strict IPA or HanLingo spelling.
- CUHK’s Cantonese character dictionary was verified to respond and exposes its frameset/readings interface. It is a linked reference, not an embedded source of automatically converted pronunciations.
- No external audio or new unlicensed photograph has been bundled.

### Chengdu: 24 local lexical forms

Primary source: Yangtian Luo, *Prosodic Phonology of the Chengdu Dialect*, University of Wisconsin–Madison, 2022, printed pp. 117–119, PDF pp. 135–137. [University-hosted dissertation](https://asset.library.wisc.edu/1711.dl/M563MSCNTS2278T/R/file-ac2d5.pdf#page=135).

The PDF was downloaded and the three original pages rendered and inspected. The new entries select compounds and reduplications directly printed with segments and meanings: directions and streets, food and utensils, descriptions and actions. Characters retain the source’s simplified writing. Aspiration superscripts become Unicode ʰ; other segment symbols remain unchanged. These pages **omit tones**. Each entry therefore uses `toneNotation: "unspecified"` and the visible label “Chengdu lexical examples · tones not supplied”. Neither citation tones nor sandhi were inferred. Whole compounds remain whole compounds; the source’s morphology examples are not relabelled as full pronunciation lessons.

Two short sound/word-formation notes and a linked reference accompany the collection. These are lexical examples rather than an unqualified claim about every Chengdu speaker.

### Guangzhou: 12 specifically attributed readings

Source: Picus Sizhi Ding, *Phonological change in Hong Kong Cantonese through language contact with Chinese topolects and English over the past century*, 2010, Table 5, printed p. 206/PDF p. 9. [Aberdeen-hosted publication](https://www.abdn.ac.uk/media/site/llmvc/documents/Ding-Phonological-change-in-Hong-Kong-Cantonese.pdf#page=9).

Downloaded the original PDF and inspected the rendered table, because its IPA font extracts as private-use glyphs. The column explicitly says **Cantonese (Guangzhou)** and credits Ting and Sun’s Sino-Tibetan Cognates Database. It is distinct from the article’s Hong Kong participants and from the adjacent Meixian, Xiamen and Suzhou columns. Imported that column’s 12 lexical facts only; retained the printed short/long vowel notation (no added length marks) and full numeric pitch values. Chinese characters identify the table’s glossed items; the table itself gives IPA and English glosses. No Hong Kong readings were copied into Guangzhou.

The two new learning notes compare nasal closures and checked syllables using those attested rows. This small comparative reference is not presented as a new Guangzhou recording sample.

### Remaining limit

Taishan remains without strict-IPA word cards. The community dictionary’s opened transcription guide explicitly simplifies multiple IPA realizations into shared letters and has accent-dependent vowels. Its spelling/audio resources remain linked; ambiguous symbols were not silently converted. The existing Chengdu vowel-raising study’s numeric token IDs were still not treated as tones.
