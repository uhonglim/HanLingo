# HanLingo editorial guide

Initial editorial record: 9 October 2026.

## Scope and language

The first edition introduces present-day Sinitic varieties through geography. Historical comparisons are deferred. English is the language of the introduction, navigation, and explanatory copy; Chinese characters identify groups, places, and example texts.

The initial selection is Mandarin 官話, Min 閩語, Yue 粵語, Hakka 客語, and Wu 吳語. These are five entry points into a larger field, not a claim that Sinitic has exactly five branches. Gan, Xiang, Jin, Hui, and other classifications are outside this edition’s coverage.

Use **Sinitic** as the scholarly label for the Han/Chinese language grouping, within Sino-Tibetan. “Han languages” is the project’s accessible framing. Do not infer a speaker’s ethnicity, nationality, or language from a map point.

## Classification decisions

Use **Sinitic → group → subgroup → local variety** as the navigation model. These are practical editorial labels rather than a claim that every authority agrees on taxonomic ranks. Some paths require additional clusters, and some classifications differ by source. Do not force every path to have the same number of levels.

Two key examples:

- **Sinitic → Min → Southern Min → Quanzhang cluster → Xiamen.** Quanzhang is a cluster spanning local varieties, not a single local dialect equivalent to Xiamen or Chengdu.
- **Sinitic → Mandarin → Southwestern Mandarin → Chengdu.** This is a deliberately shortened path; more detailed classifications may add intermediate levels.

Modern Standard Written Chinese is a supplementary written reference. It is not a sixth spoken branch or a geographically uniform pronunciation. Its formal letter must not be labeled Classical Chinese. The Mandarin letter represents colloquial Standard Mandarin; it is not a Chengdu transcription simply because Chengdu is the featured Mandarin map point.

## Introductory sound comparisons

These short descriptions identify features of named representative varieties. They are not universal definitions of each group, and a feature shared by two groups is not a unique diagnostic. In particular, the Standard Mandarin description must not be generalized to every Mandarin variety, or the Shanghai description to all Wu.

| --- | --- | --- |

## Maps

Points mark approximate city reference locations. They are not fieldwork coordinates, speaker-density measurements, complete language territories, or exclusive boundaries. Several varieties can coexist in one place, and migration extends communities well beyond the selected map points.

Use the wording: **“Points mark selected varieties, not exclusive language boundaries.”** Do not shade invented language polygons. Base-map land and administrative outlines come from `world-atlas`; these are geographic context, not linguistic classifications. The current map is a regional introduction and does not attempt a complete diaspora atlas.

## IPA and the proposed romanization

All pronunciation transcriptions must use IPA, following the [International Phonetic Association’s official chart](https://www.internationalphoneticassociation.org/IPAcharts/IPA_charts_TI/IPA_charts_TI.html). Distinguish phonetic brackets `[ ]` from phonemic slashes `/ /`, and label the exact variety and transcription convention.

The shared reading key retains these core distinctions:

| Proposed spelling | IPA illustration | Intended distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `p` | `[p]` | Voiceless unaspirated bilabial stop |
| `ph` | `[pʰ]` | Voiceless aspirated bilabial stop |
| `b` | `[b]` | Voiced bilabial stop |

HanLingo is a developing reading aid, not a phonemic standard. The same IPA receives the same spelling globally, with explicit many-to-one sound families to simplify reading. These mergers do not claim that the sounds are equivalent; source IPA preserves the differences. The current consonant, vowel, mark and tone rules are recorded in [the spelling proposal](ROMANIZATION.md). Source-category digits remain separate from pitch, and absent tones are never inferred. Source orthographies and community place names retain their documented forms. Do not imply every variety has a three-way stop contrast. In particular, an orthographic or historical “voiced” category does not guarantee a fully voiced onset in every phonetic context; see [Chen’s discussion of Shanghai](https://assets.cambridge.org/052165/2723/sample/0521652723WS.pdf).

Do not generate IPA by substituting letters in an existing romanization. Full-letter IPA and audio require a documented local reading and review. The first edition supplies neither invented recordings nor unverified full-letter transcriptions.

## Letter provenance and review

All six complete texts below were supplied by the project owner in the initial brief. Preserve them as contributed drafts until review. The geographic labels describe the intended reference, not completed verification. Review should cover vocabulary, grammar, character choices, naturalness, and the fit to the claimed local variety. The English text in the application is a shared meaning guide, not an interlinear gloss or a claim that every version says exactly the same thing.

### 1. Colloquial Standard Mandarin

媽：

我到這邊已經一個星期了，吃得好，睡得也好，你不用擔心。

昨天我和朋友去市場買東西，看見有你最愛吃的餅，就買了一盒，打算帶回家給你。

這幾天有點冷，你出門記得多穿件衣服。我下個月有空就回家，到時候坐下來，陪你慢慢聊。

想你的兒子

### 2. Min, intended Xiamen Southern Min reference

阿母：

我來遮已經一個禮拜矣，食睏攏好，你毋免煩惱。

昨昏我佮朋友去菜市仔買物件，看著你上愛食的餅，就買一盒，想欲提轉去厝予你。

這幾工有淡薄仔寒，你出門愛記得加穿一領衫。下個月我若有閒就轉去厝，閣坐落來，陪你慢慢仔開講。

想你的囝

### 3. Wu, intended Shanghai reference

姆媽：

我到搿搭已經一個禮拜了，吃得蠻好，睏得也蠻好，儂勿要擔心。

昨日我搭朋友一道去菜場買物事，看見有儂頂歡喜吃個餅，就買了一盒，打算帶回屋裏撥儂。

搿幾日有眼冷，儂出門記得多穿件衣裳。我下個月有空就回屋裏，再坐下來陪儂慢慢講閒話。

想儂個兒子

### 4. Yue, intended Guangfu Cantonese reference

阿媽：

我嚟呢邊已經一個禮拜喇，食得好，瞓得又好，你唔使擔心。

尋日我同朋友去街市買嘢，見到有你最鍾意食嘅餅，就買咗一盒，諗住帶返屋企畀你。

呢幾日有啲凍，你出門口記得着多件衫。我下個月得閒就返屋企，到時再坐低陪你慢慢傾偈。

掛住你嘅仔

### 5. Hakka, intended Meixian reference

阿姆：

𠊎來這邊有一隻禮拜哩，食好睡好，你毋使愁。

昨晡日𠊎同朋友去市場買東西，看著你最愛食个餅，就買一盒，打算帶轉屋下分你。

這幾日天氣冷，你出門記得着多一件衫。下隻月𠊎有閒就轉屋下，再坐下來陪你慢慢講。

想你个倈仔

### 6. Modern Standard Written Chinese, formal register

親愛的媽媽：

我抵達這裡已有一週，飲食起居一切安好，請您放心。

昨天，我與朋友到市場購物，看見您喜愛的餅，便買了一盒，準備回家時帶給您。

近日天氣轉涼，外出時請記得添衣。下個月如有空閒，我會回家探望您，陪您坐下來，慢慢聊聊。

想念您的兒子

## Future evidence model

Geography remains the active dimension. When historical comparison is added, each attestation should retain its own provenance instead of assigning one imaginary historical pronunciation to an entire group.

Recommended fields for a future record:

- Stable variety identifier, local name, and source-specific classification path.
- Locality and geographic precision; distinguish a city reference from a speaker’s actual community.
- Attestation date or date range, with uncertainty; separate publication, recording, collection, and represented-language dates.
- Source title, author/contributor, stable URL or bibliographic reference, exact page/item, and reuse rights.
- Original text, editorial changes, translation, and register.
- IPA convention, citation versus connected-speech forms, tone and sandhi treatment, and any audio reference.
- Evidence kind: contemporary recording, documentary transcription, or scholarly reconstruction. Mark reconstructions explicitly.
- Review status, review scope, reviewer attribution where authorized, and revision date.

“Hakka 200 years ago” is insufficient as an entry. A useful historical record must identify which Hakka variety, where, approximately when, and the evidence for that claim. Present-day examples also vary by speaker, generation, and context; the map should not erase that variation.
