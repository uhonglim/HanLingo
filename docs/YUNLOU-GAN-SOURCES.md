## Exact language and geographic scope

The paper names **Ji’an Yunlou Gan Chinese** on p.2 and calls it **Ji–Cha Gan** on p.4 footnote2. It also documents the local label 新安声 “Xin’an accent”. These facts justify the existing `gan/jicha/jicha-localities` parent; the final collection level is editorial geography, not an extra source-defined linguistic subgroup.

P.2 footnote1 says data mainly come from elicited/introspective judgments of one native-speaker author. Three additional native speakers cross-check specified grammaticality judgments. A collection year, speaker ages, addresses and village-level survey boundary are not supplied; **2026 is publication year**. Neither an urban Ji’an accent nor a whole-county uniform language is implied.

New locality `yunlou-gan` uses the separately sourced [GeoNames populated-place record1923997](https://www.geonames.org/1923997/yunlou.html): Yunlou, Jiangxi, Ji’an Shi, WGS84 **26.86083N,115.16583E**. GeoNames [licenses its gazetteer CC BY4.0](https://www.geonames.org/about.html). This is a settlement reference near the named former Yunlou area, not an inferred consultant address or linguistic boundary. No existing Yunlou atlas entry was found; existing Ji’an references remain intact. Do not substitute Futian’s town centre or a Ji’an city point.

## Selection and writing status

Eight records explicitly pair source Han and IPA: 来、去、来去、连、要、应该、把、得. Twelve more source examples supply a pronunciation and English gloss **without a written form**. Their `han` is null and `writingStatus` is `not-supplied`; Chinese characters must not be filled in by translating the gloss. This is source omission, not a claim that the local word cannot be written.

All20 are lexical/grammatical forms with source meaning and `learningKind: word`, rather than homophone-table character readings. Each is labelled **Published sentence-example form**. Pronunciations selected from the sentence are not asserted to be independently measured isolated-word citation forms. The compound 来去 has both literal motion and fused grammatical readings; it is not an unconditional equivalent of English “let’s”. 把 and 得 are syntax particles in this source, not unsupported meanings “hold” and “get”.

PDF text extraction uses font placeholders (`@`, `C`, `N`) and must not be copied directly. Visual review establishes **ə,ɕ,ŋ,ɛ**; superscript aspiration h is encoded as modifierʰ. Typographic word hyphens become spaces, while source tone digits remain unchanged as Unicode superscripts. The ledger explains these typography changes.

Held out: the reduced luai213 ə0 form (0 is not an invented sixth pitch level), compounds with explicit tone-change chains such as school55–51/324–55 and bottle324–24. Neither sandhi chains nor missing citation information are silently reconstructed. No absent audio is claimed.

## Cultural context

[Local gazetteer heritage entries in the Knowledge Granular Resources Library](https://meta.librarydata.cn/bookview?gid=140335020220000201&sid=140310020220000002) explicitly distinguish:

- 百岁堂 **Baisui Hall**, Yunlou village in Futian: historical record dated1273, associated with Luo Geng’s grandmother’s hundredth birthday and Wen Tianxiang’s celebratory poem.
- 张氏宗祠 **Zhang ancestral hall**, Luxia in the Yunlou area: record of an inscribed couplet by Kuang Ruxie of Zhixia.

The two brief notes paraphrase bounded geographic/historical facts. No extended poem, gazetteer prose or scan is reproduced. Neither note claims present preservation or identifies a pronunciation recording location. The original online book display was not reliably accessible directly at review time; these exact entries were exposed by the source’s search-indexed text. No photograph is inferred from them.
