## Word evidence and normalization

- **Meixian:** [Cheung, _Vowels and Tones in Meixian Hakka_](https://lbms03.cityu.edu.hk/theses/c_ftt/phd-ctl-b40860632f.pdf), Table 2.1, printed p. 19 / PDF page 36, visually checked. The study's ten speakers grew up in Meijiang and were 50–70. Words keep that table's broad segments and numeric pitch values. The source discusses different tone transcriptions elsewhere; we do not merge those into this table. Traditional characters replace simplified typographic forms without changing readings. Source superscript aspiration is represented with IPA ʰ.
- **Lufeng:** [Lü Wan-yun, _客語《陸豐方言》的百年語言演變析探_](https://cloud.hakka.gov.tw/Attachment/1/84178533971.pdf), printed/PDF pp. 106–107. This table explicitly labels the Guangdong Lufeng column as August 2007 fieldwork and the comparison column as Hsinchu, Taiwan. Three forms are taken only from the Guangdong column: 老弟, 老妹, 湖鰍. Page 107 was visually checked. Source apostrophe aspiration is normalized to IPA ʰ; displayed contours are the table's two-digit values, not tone-category indices. The precise town and speakers for this table are not provided there, so this uncertainty is visible in each reading note. No 1897 historical forms have been passed off as contemporary.
- **Shanghai:** [Chen & Gussenhoven 2015, _Shanghai Chinese_](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/E58F14205E5EFF63067C6A180DB7AEEA/S0025100315000043a.pdf/div-class-title-shanghai-chinese-div.pdf), pp. 322–328. The authors use tone-category indices in most examples. We explicitly use their footnote 2 proposed citation-tone letters: T1 ˥˩, T2 ˧˥, T3 ˨˩˧, T4 ˦, T5 ˨˩˧. These become numeric contours 51, 35, 213, 4, 213. This is an author-documented mapping, not an inferred conversion from category number. Broad consonant symbols remain broad: the 'voiced' series does not imply continuous voicing initially. Speaker born in the 1950s, raised in Huangpu. No claim that this is a universal Shanghai standard.
- **Wenzhou:** [Franziska Scholz 2012, _Tone sandhi, prosodic phrasing, and focus marking in Wenzhou Chinese_](https://www.lotpublications.nl/Documents/305_fulltext.pdf), Chapter 2 pp. 14–20, especially the word tables on pp. 15 and 20, both visually checked. The description concerns young Lucheng speakers, with the phonology sketch mainly based on two 25-year-old women. IPA glyphs were checked against rendered pages because PDF text extraction corrupts many glyphs. Pitch numbers are already explicit Chao contours. Traditional character equivalents are used. No reconstructions from a romanization system.

Every current word is marked `toneNotation: 'pitch-contour'`. No recording is fabricated or embedded without separate media rights.

## Sound and resource limits

- **Changting:** [Lin 2007](https://toaj.stpi.niar.org.tw/index/journal/volume/article/4b1141f987fa79a3018808aead6d1f34) provides a primary fieldwork abstract on two- and three-syllable sandhi; the download requires verification. [NCU Chengguan thesis](https://etd.lib.ncu.edu.tw/detail/58397520cd865be401a7419ea86e6768/?seq=3) identifies locality and research scope. These support the sound notes and study links, but no verified complete word-plus-contour table was extracted. Words remain empty deliberately.
- **Lishui:** [Lan, Chen & Zhang 2023](https://www.internationalphoneticassociation.org/icphs-proceedings/ICPhS2023/full_papers/542.pdf) identifies Liandu urban speakers and reports variation in tone sandhi. It does not include a lexical stimulus table sufficient for word lessons. [Steed 2010 ANU record](https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/items/69a0c01b-9f60-4eef-8e5d-cc0aed96accc) is linked for its locality-specific thesis; the server intermittently failed in direct retrieval. Words remain empty.
- **Suzhou:** [Feng 2007](https://www.icphs2007.de/conference/Papers/1321/1321.pdf) supports the specific fricative-vowel note; Shanghai words are not assigned to Suzhou.
- **Taiwan Hakka resources:** [MOE dictionary](https://hakkadict.moe.edu.tw/) and [Taiwan Hailu overview](https://language.moe.gov.tw/001/Upload/Files/site_content/M0001/hkknowledge/01/107_004_%E8%87%BA%E7%81%A3%E6%B5%B7%E9%99%B8%E8%85%94%E5%AE%A2%E5%AE%B6%E8%AA%9E%E7%B0%A1%E4%BB%8B.pdf) are branch-level comparative resources with no mainland locality binding. Dictionary domain retrieval intermittently timed out, but official search results and the existing source catalog confirm it. Neither supplies the Guangdong word entries.

## Culture scope

Culture sources are linked on every item. Meixian housing is Nankou/Qiaoxiang; Hailu salted tea is explicitly a regional practice across communities; Changting dried tofu is supported by Fujian's heritage list item 104; Suzhou Pingtan and Kunqu use local cultural-office and UNESCO documentation; Wenzhou printing concerns Rui’an and is explicitly separated from Lucheng pronunciation; Lishui Tongji Weir is in Liandu, southwest of the city center. These do not claim that everyone shown or discussed shares one pronunciation.

UNESCO's descriptive pages were available through indexed canonical records, although some direct URLs served an anti-bot challenge during verification. No images or film from those pages were copied. The Zhu Yuhao tutorial is linked as community-authored study material; its CC BY-NC-ND text is not reproduced or converted into HanLingo lessons.
