# Wu and Hakka atlas references

Checked 2026-10-09. The catalogue adds named reference places, **not** newly verified pronunciations, photographs or full learning packs. There are 97 entries: 62 Wu and 35 Hakka. All 15 previously published Wu/Hakka locality IDs are retained.

## What the hierarchy means

Every entry has group → branch → cluster → locality navigation. A `classification` cluster follows an actual subdivision in the cited source. A `geographic` cluster is an explicitly labelled browsing collection; it is not another claimed genetic level. The source's own rank and date remain in reference notes. Equal navigation depth must not imply that every source uses equivalent taxonomic ranks.

Documented place spellings are preferred. Where a checked community romanization is unavailable, these entries retain established geographic spellings and Chinese names. They do not manufacture endonyms or claim geographic spellings are HanLingo pronunciation.

## Wu

### Zhejiang primary survey

[Fu Guotong, Fang Songxi, Cai Yongfei, Bao Shijie and Fu Zuozhi, *Zhejiang Wu classification*, Zhejiang Linguistics Society, 1985](https://zjjcmspublic.oss-cn-hangzhou-zwynet-d01-a.internet.cloud.zj.gov.cn/jcms_files/jcms1/web2476/site/attach/0/16hywh-2/%E6%B5%99%E6%B1%9F%E5%90%B4%E8%AF%AD%E5%88%86%E5%8C%BA.pdf).

The official gazetteer-hosted original is an image scan. The cover, title page and classification paragraphs were inspected visually, rather than relying on corrupted extraction. PDF page numbers are printed page numbers + 6.

This is a dated classification reference, not a survey of current speaker numbers, borders or every resident's speech. Its word transcriptions were not imported as new lesson IPA.

### Later Zhejiang gazetteer

The provincial [*Dialect Gazetteer*, digital page 49](https://dfz.zj.gov.cn/zlyz/ossfs/h5/ZS-H-330000-2010-097-0101/files/basic-html/page49.html) places Hangzhou, Jiaxing, Huzhou, Ningbo and Linshao within the Zhejiang portion of Taihu Wu. More specific prose lists were read directly:

- [Page 51](https://dfz.zj.gov.cn/zlyz/ossfs/h5/ZS-H-330000-2010-097-0101/files/basic-html/page51.html): old urban Hangzhou is distinguished from Yuhang and from named surrounding villages.
- [Page 169](https://dfz.zj.gov.cn/zlyz/ossfs/h5/ZS-H-330000-2010-097-0101/files/basic-html/page169.html): Ningbo/Mingzhou/Yongjiang distribution; Ninghai's Chalu division matters.
- [Page 208](https://dfz.zj.gov.cn/zlyz/ossfs/h5/ZS-H-330000-2010-097-0101/files/basic-html/page208.html): Zhangting and Lubu differ from the Linshao classification of the rest of Yuyao.
- [Page 346](https://dfz.zj.gov.cn/zlyz/ossfs/h5/ZS-H-330000-2010-097-0101/files/basic-html/page346.html): Lishui subdivision, with Qingtian and Taishun exceptions.
- [Page 381](https://dfz.zj.gov.cn/zlyz/ossfs/h5/ZS-H-330000-2010-097-0101/files/basic-html/page381.html): five Quzhou reference areas.

The newer gazetteer's Lishui/Quzhou split is used instead of extending the older monograph's differing assignments. The original book and later gazetteer do not supply interchangeable classification schemes.

### Jiangsu and Shanghai

- [Taicang cultural bureau, 2020](https://www.taicang.gov.cn/taicang/tcwh/202009/ddacdc104e204cb58023f837d3591499.shtml), opening paragraph: Taihu → Su-Hu-Jia and named nearby speech references.
- [Shanghai culture and tourism authority](https://cmp.whlyj.sh.gov.cn/CMP/sho_init.ac?type=4), “上海方言”: urban Shanghai's Taihu/Su-Hu-Jia placement.
- [Wuxi archives, 2014](https://daj.wuxi.gov.cn/doc/2014/06/10/2426144.shtml): Suzhou as the representative Su-Hu-Jia reference. The article also stresses Wuxi's transition position; this dataset does not flatten that issue into a new Wuxi record.

Jiaxing references are placed under Su-Hu-Jia in the broader atlas convention, consistent with the 1985 monograph’s classification discussion. The provincial gazetteer’s finer Jiaxing subdivision remains source context rather than an extra or overlapping navigation layer.

## Hakka

### Mainland local references

The existing Yue–Tai, Hailu and Tingzhou parent routes are retained. Geographic collections below them do not add unsourced genetic subdivisions.

- [Dabu county, 2024](https://www.dabu.gov.cn/zjdp/whts/fy/content/post_2591731.html), “大埔客家方言的分片”: five internal areas. Four newly catalogued towns plus the existing Huliao/Dabu reference are specifically named. Older merged township names are not silently treated as current independent towns.
- [NCU Hakka College, 2012](https://hakka.ncu.edu.tw/Hakka_ePaper/paper/paper150/02_01.html): report of Wu Chung-chieh's research, describing Hetian and Xintian groupings and migration evidence. Taiwan Hailu must not be equated mechanically with one mainland county. Nanwan's contact situation is discussed but not reduced to an uncomplicated extra catalogue point.
- [Hsu's Wuhua research abstract, 2011](https://hakka.ncu.edu.tw/Hakka_ePaper/paper/paper139/05_03.html): northern/southern phonological and east/west lexical divisions. The existing Wuhua marker remains an approximate county-town reference, not a universal Wuhua reading.
- [Meixian dissertation, City University of Hong Kong](https://lbms03.cityu.edu.hk/theses/c_ftt/phd-ctl-b40860632f.pdf) and [Hakka Affairs Council Xingning study](https://sign.hakka.gov.tw/File/Get?filename=%5CAttach%5C1990%5C1%5C8522162371.pdf): retained sources for previously published city entries; no new forms extracted.
- [Lin Hui-shan, 2007, *Changting Hakka Tone Sandhi*](https://toaj.stpi.niar.org.tw/index/journal/volume/article/4b1141f987fa79a3018808aead6d1f34), Chinese abstract and pp. 175–225: county town and suburbs, not every locality in Changting.

### Taiwan Hailu localities

[Taichung Hakka Affairs Commission, “客語腔調分布”](https://www.hakka.taichung.gov.tw/28009/28168/71488/381494/), **海陸腔** list. Eighteen listed locality references are included. The editorial “Taiwan Hailu” collection is geographic. Guanxi's partial distribution is visible; the list does not identify all residents as Hailu speakers. Yangmei is omitted in this selection rather than forcing its overlapping accent references into one leaf. Other Taiwan Hakka accents are not claimed to be covered by this collection.

## Map anchors and limits

Coordinates are rounded browsing anchors for named places, not survey-speaker coordinates, language borders or whole-municipality labels. Dabu town anchors were cross-checked against OpenStreetMap place or town-office results through Nominatim; the checked records are kept in the ignored `.evidence/atlas-wu-hakka/` working folder. Coordinates are not represented as measurements from the linguistic publications.

Haifeng and Lufeng retain their legacy county anchors for route continuity; their visible scope explicitly warns that these do not identify the mostly Min-speaking county towns as Hakka references. The new named Luhe towns are more specific references.

No new galleries, learner words, audio, dialect IPA or automatic pronunciation are inferred from this catalogue. The 15 existing locality pages keep their existing evidence and learning material.
