# Yue locality expansion

Research and implementation: 9 October 2026.

## Scope

Adds four conventional atlas branches — Yong–Xun, Qin–Lian, Gao–Yang and Wu–Hua — and nine locality references: Macau, Foshan, Kaiping, Jiangmen, Nanning, Wuzhou, Beihai, Yangjiang and Huazhou. Combined with the existing four localities, Yue now has 13 reference places in seven branches.

The grouping follows the traditional Language Atlas classification summarized in Ho Wang Matthew Sung's _Advancing Explanatory and Tonal Dialectometry_ (2026), chapter 2, especially printed pages 14–17. The same thesis explains alternative classifications and their limitations. The tree is an editorial navigation choice, not a claim that this taxonomy is uniquely settled.

- Urban Nanning: Yong–Xun Yue, not a merged entry for Pinghua or Zhuang.
- Urban Wuzhou: Guangfu. Surrounding rural references can belong to Goulou.
- Kaiping and Jiangmen: separate Siyi survey points. Neither page represents its entire municipal jurisdiction.
- Beihai: Qin–Lian. Do not silently merge it with Qinzhou or Lianzhou.
- Yangjiang: Gao–Yang, kept apart from named Yangchun sources.
- Huazhou: Wu–Hua 吳化, not Hakka Wuhua 五華. The comparative phonology source specifically uses Xiajiang material; the overview retains this qualification.

Names are current English locality identifiers used in the cited research or institutional pages. No new community-language pronunciation has been invented from characters. Documented endonyms can replace visible labels later while preserving stable route IDs.

## What is and is not provided

Each locality has an article, two learning notes, two documented culture topics, resources and a licensed photo gallery. Learning notes explain actual source comparisons and how to keep locality scope accurate. This expansion does not manufacture word readings, IPA, pitch values or recordings to open empty practice pages. Vocabulary depth remains a separate research task.

Cultural images can include precisely captioned nearby places within the wider municipality, such as Chikan around Kaiping or Hailing Island around Yangjiang. They are contextual photographs, not speech samples. A city marker does not assert that every depicted person speaks the named variety.

## Evidence

- [Sung 2026, thesis](https://www.lotpublications.nl/Documents/709_fulltext.pdf): classification and survey locality distinctions.
- [Sung, Chan and Lin 2023](https://doi.org/10.3390/languages8020146): named Yue survey sites and contact-related rhyme comparisons.
- [Lee and Lo 2026](https://journal.kci.go.kr/ksclc/archive/articleView?artiId=ART003308222): three-character changed-tone patterns, including Macau and Foshan.
- [Tan 2017, Ohio State dissertation](https://etd.ohiolink.edu/acprod/odb_etd/r/etd/search/10?clear=10&p10_accession_num=osu1502688160423343): phonological and lexical comparisons of seven Siyi localities.
- [Liu 2012, HKUST thesis abstract](https://ccl.hkust.edu.hk/files/graduate/mphil_abstracts/LIU%20Xiao%202012.pdf): phonetic tone study including Kaiping.
- UNESCO, local government and cultural/industry institutions support cultural topics. Each item links its source directly.

## Photograph provenance

Every photograph was retrieved from its individual Wikimedia Commons file page. The data preserves the credited creator, a specific reuse license, the source URL and an accurate scene caption. Files are stored as `public/images/expansion-yue-*.webp`. Contact sheets were visually checked; repeated viewpoints and multiple photos of the same bakery counter are excluded from the published selection. Local reference metadata and processing evidence are under the ignored `.evidence/yue-expansion/` directory.
